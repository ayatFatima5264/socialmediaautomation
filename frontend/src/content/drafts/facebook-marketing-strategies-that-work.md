Facebook has been declared dead roughly once a year for a decade. Meanwhile it remains one of the largest platforms on earth, and for entire categories of business — local services, community-driven brands, anything selling to people over 35 — it quietly outperforms the platforms that get more attention.

What actually happened is narrower than "Facebook died." Organic reach for business Pages fell dramatically, and the tactics that worked in 2015 stopped working. Businesses that kept doing those tactics concluded the platform was finished. Businesses that adapted did fine.

This guide covers what still works: the strategies, the format changes, and where to put a small budget if you have one.

## What Actually Changed

Understanding this determines everything else.

Facebook's feed shifted heavily toward content from friends, family, and Groups, and away from Pages broadcasting to followers. A Page post now reaches a small fraction of the people who follow it, and that fraction has been falling for years.

The mistake is reading that as "Facebook does not work." The accurate reading is: **Facebook stopped being a free broadcast channel and became a community and distribution platform.** Those require different tactics.

The businesses still succeeding organically are not fighting this. They are operating where the attention actually went — Groups, comments, personal profiles, and content that gets shared between people rather than pushed from a Page.

## Who Facebook Is Genuinely Good For

Be honest about fit before investing effort.

**Strong fit:**

- Local businesses — trades, restaurants, clinics, salons, retail with a physical location
- Community-driven brands and membership organisations
- Businesses selling to audiences over roughly 35
- Event-based businesses
- Anything where local recommendations drive purchases

**Weaker fit:**

- Youth-focused consumer brands
- B2B software and professional services, where LinkedIn is usually stronger
- Visual-first fashion and design brands, where Instagram tends to work harder

If you are in the second group, do not force it. One platform done well beats three done thinly.

## Strategy 1: Groups Are Where the Reach Went

This is the single biggest organic opportunity on Facebook and most businesses ignore it.

Group content still reaches members reliably, because Facebook treats Groups as the community layer it wants to promote. There are two ways to use this.

**Participate in existing local Groups.** Find the community groups where your customers already are — local town groups, parenting groups, trade groups, hobby groups. Then genuinely participate. Answer questions in your area of expertise without pitching. Over months, you become the person people tag when someone asks for a recommendation.

This is slow, unscalable, and extremely effective for local businesses. One plumber answering questions properly in three local groups will out-generate most paid campaigns at the same effort cost.

**Rules that matter:** read the group rules, never post promotional content unless explicitly allowed, and answer more than you ask. Groups eject self-promoters fast, and the reputational damage is real.

**Run your own Group.** Harder, but valuable if you have a genuine community — customers of a gym, owners of a product, members of a local interest. A Group you own is an audience you can reach, which a Page increasingly is not.

## Strategy 2: Content That Gets Shared, Not Just Seen

Since Page reach is limited, the only reliable amplification is people sharing your content into their own feeds.

**What actually gets shared on Facebook:**

- **Genuinely useful local information.** Road closures, opening hours over holidays, local events, weather-related advice. Unglamorous, heavily shared.
- **Community recognition.** Celebrating a local team, a customer's milestone, a staff member's achievement.
- **Before and afters.** Visible transformation is the most naturally shareable format there is.
- **Warnings and heads-ups.** "If you have one of these, check this." Highly shared when genuine.
- **Nostalgia and local history.** Reliably strong in community groups and local Pages.
- **Genuinely funny content about your industry.** Hard to do well, very effective when it lands.

**What does not get shared:** product announcements, "we are excited to share," generic tips, and anything that reads like an advertisement.

## Strategy 3: Facebook Is Still a Search and Verification Tool

A large number of people check a business's Facebook Page before buying — not to read your posts, but to verify you exist, see your opening hours, read reviews, and check that you posted recently.

This means your Page has a job even if nobody engages with it.

**Make sure:**

- Opening hours, address, phone, and website are accurate. This is the most common and most costly oversight.
- Your most recent post is recent. A Page whose last post was eight months ago actively damages credibility.
- Reviews and recommendations are enabled, and you reply to all of them.
- Your cover image and about section clearly say what you do and where.

For a local business, getting these right is worth more than any content strategy.

## Strategy 4: Reviews and Recommendations

Facebook recommendations function as local social proof and often surface when people ask for suggestions in groups.

**What to do:**

- **Ask.** Most satisfied customers will leave one if asked directly at the right moment — just after a good outcome.
- **Reply to every review**, positive and negative. Reference something specific from what they said; a generic thank-you is nearly worthless.
- **Handle negatives properly.** A calm, specific, non-defensive reply to a bad review persuades more readers than ten positive reviews. Never argue publicly.

## Strategy 5: Video and Reels Still Get Distribution

Facebook pushes short-form video hard, and it remains one of the few ways a Page can reach beyond its existing followers organically.

**Practical guidance:**

- Simple phone video outperforms polished production for local and service businesses.
- Front-load the hook. The first two seconds decide everything.
- Caption everything — most viewing is silent.
- Repurpose. A clip made for Instagram Reels works on Facebook with minimal change.

## Strategy 6: Small, Targeted Paid Spend

Facebook advertising remains genuinely effective and is unusually accessible at small budgets — this is one area where the platform has not declined.

If you have limited money, spend it here in this order:

**1. Boost your best organic post to a local audience.** If a post already earned shares organically, that is evidence it resonates. Boosting proven content to a tight geographic radius is the highest-return small spend available.

**2. Retarget website visitors.** People who already visited your site are the warmest audience you have and the cheapest to reach.

**3. A simple local awareness campaign** with a clear, specific offer — not brand awareness.

**What to avoid:** broad untargeted campaigns, boosting everything indiscriminately, and complex funnels before you have proven a single message works.

## Strategy 7: Cross-Post Intelligently, Not Identically

Most businesses running Instagram and Facebook publish identical content to both. It works better than nothing, but it leaves value on the table.

Facebook tolerates and rewards different things:

- **Longer captions work.** Facebook readers will read more than Instagram readers.
- **Links are viable.** Unlike Instagram, you can drive traffic directly.
- **Community framing outperforms aspiration.** "Anyone else dealing with this?" beats a polished statement.
- **Fewer hashtags.** They matter far less on Facebook; two or three is plenty.

Adapting takes one instruction with an AI tool and about a minute manually. Worth doing.

## Strategy 8: Events and Local Discovery

Facebook Events remain genuinely underused, and they get distribution that ordinary Page posts do not — attendees' friends see their interest, which is organic reach you cannot otherwise buy.

Worth creating an Event for:

- Anything with a date: a sale, a workshop, an open day, a launch
- Recurring things — a weekly market stall, a monthly class
- Anything you are participating in rather than hosting, where you can co-host with the organiser

**Practical tips:** create the Event at least two weeks ahead, add a clear image with the date readable on it, post updates inside the Event as the date approaches (attendees get notified), and always co-host with any partner business — it doubles the reach at no cost.

Even for businesses that do not run events, framing something as one occasionally is a legitimate way to access that distribution.

## What to Track on Facebook

Keep it simple. The Page dashboard offers far more numbers than are useful.

**Worth watching:**

- **Shares.** The clearest signal that content resonated, and the only real organic amplification available to a Page.
- **Reach from non-followers.** Tells you whether anything is escaping your existing audience.
- **Review count and rating**, trending over months.
- **Enquiries and calls** that came through the Page.

**Safe to ignore:** Page likes, total impressions in isolation, and the performance of any single post. Variance is high — look at direction over a quarter.

For local businesses specifically, the most useful metric is not on Facebook at all: ask every new customer where they heard about you and count the answers.

## Common Facebook Mistakes

- **Treating the Page as a broadcast channel.** Organic Page reach is limited by design now. Build reach through Groups, shares, and video.
- **Neglecting basic information.** Wrong opening hours cost more real business than any content mistake.
- **Ignoring reviews.** Especially negative ones. Silence reads as indifference.
- **Self-promoting in Groups.** Fastest way to get removed and remembered badly.
- **Abandoning the Page but leaving it up.** Worse than not having one.
- **Boosting posts indiscriminately.** Boost proven content only.
- **Assuming it does not work without testing.** For local businesses in particular, Facebook frequently outperforms platforms that get more attention.

## A Realistic Weekly Rhythm

For a local business:

- **2–3 Page posts a week** — mix of useful local information, behind the scenes, customer results, and one promotional post.
- **1 short video or Reel a week**, repurposed from Instagram if you use it.
- **15 minutes daily in Groups** — genuinely answering questions, not promoting.
- **Reply to every review and comment.**
- **Monthly:** check your Page information is still accurate, and boost the best-performing organic post from the previous month to a local audience.

Around three hours a week, most of it in Groups and replies rather than content production.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Facebook still worth it for businesses in 2026?

For local businesses, community-driven brands, and audiences over roughly 35, yes — often more than platforms that receive more attention. What changed is that organic Page reach declined sharply, so the free-broadcast approach stopped working. Businesses succeeding on Facebook now operate through Groups, shareable content, video, and small targeted ad spend rather than posting to a Page and hoping.

### Why has my Facebook Page reach dropped so much?

Facebook shifted its feed heavily toward content from friends, family, and Groups, and away from Pages broadcasting to followers. This is a structural change rather than a penalty on your account. The response is to build reach through channels the platform still favours: participating in Groups, creating content people share into their own feeds, posting short video, and boosting proven posts.

### Should my business join Facebook Groups or create one?

Start by joining existing Groups where your customers already are — local community groups are usually the highest-value for service businesses. Participate genuinely by answering questions in your area of expertise without pitching, and read the rules carefully, since self-promotion gets you removed quickly. Creating your own Group is worthwhile only when you have a real community with a reason to gather.

### How much should a small business spend on Facebook ads?

Start small and spend on proven content. The highest-return approach at low budget is boosting a post that already earned organic shares to a tightly targeted local audience, followed by retargeting people who visited your website. Avoid broad awareness campaigns and complex funnels until you have evidence that a specific message resonates.

### Can I post the same content to Facebook and Instagram?

You can, and it is better than posting nothing, but adapting improves results noticeably. Facebook readers tolerate longer captions, links work directly, community-oriented framing outperforms polished statements, and hashtags matter far less — two or three is plenty compared to Instagram. The adaptation takes about a minute per post manually and is instant with an AI tool.

## The Bottom Line

Facebook did not stop working. It stopped being free reach.

For local and community-driven businesses, it remains one of the most effective platforms available — provided you operate where the attention actually is: in Groups, in shareable content, in reviews, in video, and in small, well-targeted paid spend on things that already proved themselves.

Fix your Page information this week. Join three local Groups and answer questions for a month. Reply to every review. That alone will outperform most content strategies.
